Forward, futures, and security

You will be able to
Distinguish a forward contract, a futures contract, and ownership of a security from a short fact pattern.

All three can change value with a market price, but they create different rights, obligations, and counterparty structures.

ForwardPrivate • customized • bilateral credit
FuturesStandardized • exchange-traded • cleared
SecurityOften ownership or creditor claim

The idea

A forward is a privately negotiated agreement between counterparties. Quantity, quality, date, and place can be customized, but the parties face each other's credit and liquidity risk.

A futures contract is standardized by an exchange and processed through a clearing system. Both buyer and seller have obligations. Positions are marked to market, and most are offset rather than held to delivery.

Buying common stock normally transfers an ownership interest. Buying a futures contract does not mean the long has paid for or owns the underlying commodity. Futures margin is a performance bond, not a down payment. A long futures position has an obligation to accept the contract's settlement outcome unless it is offset.

Read the contract clues

  1. A coffee roaster privately agrees with one farm on a unique grade, quantity, and delivery warehouse.
  2. The customization and direct counterparty relationship indicate a forward.
  3. If the roaster instead buys a standardized exchange contract cleared through a clearinghouse, it is a futures position.
Immediate retrieval

Check the model

1. Which feature most strongly identifies a futures contract?
Standardization and clearing distinguish exchange-traded futures from bilateral forwards.
2. A long futures position is best described as…
Both futures sides carry obligations; an option buyer holds a right.
3. Which instrument most directly exposes two named parties to each other's credit?
A forward is bilateral; clearing interposes a clearinghouse for futures.
Transfer problem

Use it somewhere new

A jeweler wants exactly 2,350 ounces of a particular alloy delivered to its own plant on a nonstandard date. Which contract form fits the customization, and what risk becomes more direct?

A private forward fits the custom terms. Bilateral counterparty credit and liquidity risk become more direct than in a standardized cleared futures contract.
