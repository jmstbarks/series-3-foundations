Turn a quote into dollars

You will be able to
Calculate tick value and futures profit or loss from stated contract specifications.

The exam's most reusable move is translating a quoted price change into contract dollars.

price change×contract unit×contracts=dollar P/L

The idea

A tick is the contract's minimum quoted price movement. Tick value = tick size × contract unit, after correctly interpreting the quote. Contract specifications control the conversion.

For a long, P/L = (exit price − entry price) × unit × contracts. For a short, reverse the price difference: (entry − exit) × unit × contracts. Subtract commissions and fees when asked for net profit.

Do not divide by margin to calculate dollar profit. Margin affects leverage and return on equity, not the contract's dollar price change.

Long futures P/L

  1. Simplified contract: 5,000 units; tick = $0.0025 per unit. Tick value = 5,000 × $0.0025 = $12.50.
  2. Buy at $6.1000 and sell at $6.1800: price change = $0.0800.
  3. Gross profit = $0.0800 × 5,000 = $400. Equivalently, 32 ticks × $12.50.
Immediate retrieval

Check the model

1. A contract covers 2,000 units and its minimum move is $0.005. Tick value is…
2,000 × $0.005 = $10.
2. A trader shorts one 1,000-unit contract at 54.20 and offsets at 53.70. Gross result?
Short P/L = (54.20 − 53.70) × 1,000 = $500 profit.
3. Two contracts each gain $375 before $40 total commissions. Net profit is…
Gross is 2 × $375 = $750; subtract $40 = $710.
Transfer problem

Use it somewhere new

A 50-unit contract moves from 1,982.5 to 1,976.0. Calculate one-contract P/L for a short and for a long.

The move is 6.5 points × 50 = $325. The short gains $325; the long loses $325.
