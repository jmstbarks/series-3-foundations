Module 4 · Trading applications · Lesson 12 of 21

Construct a speculative futures trade

You will be able to
Given a directional view and risk constraint, choose the position, protective order, and calculate net return on margin.

A view is not a complete trade until direction, size, exit, risk, and cost are explicit.

View→Long / short→Protective order→Net P/L ÷ margin

The idea

A bullish futures view maps to a long position; a bearish view maps to a short position. The position is leveraged, so a small price move relative to notional value can be large relative to margin.

A protective sell stop can be used below a long; a protective buy stop can be used above a short. Stops do not guarantee the trigger price, especially through gaps or locked-limit conditions.

Gross P/L comes from price movement × unit × contracts. Net P/L subtracts commissions and fees. Return on margin equity = net P/L ÷ stated margin investment. Ensure the question's denominator and whether commissions are round-turn or per side.

Net result and return

  1. Buy two contracts at 118.20 and sell at 119.05. Unit = 1,000; total commissions = $80.
  2. Gross = 0.85 × 1,000 × 2 = $1,700. Net = $1,700 − $80 = $1,620.
  3. If total margin invested was $9,000, return = $1,620 ÷ $9,000 = 18%.
Immediate retrieval

Check the model

1. A bearish speculator normally initiates…
A short benefits from a futures price decline.
2. A protective order for a short position is commonly…
The short is harmed by a rise; a buy stop can activate above the market.
3. Net profit is $900 on $6,000 margin. Return on margin is…
$900 ÷ $6,000 = 15%.
Transfer problem

Use it somewhere new

A short earns $1,250 gross, pays $70 total costs, and used $5,900 margin. Calculate net profit and return on margin.

Net profit is $1,180. Return is $1,180 ÷ $5,900 = 20%.
